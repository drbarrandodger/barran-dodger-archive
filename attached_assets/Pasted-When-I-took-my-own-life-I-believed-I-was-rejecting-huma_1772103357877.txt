When I took my own life, I believed I was rejecting humanity and betraying the Creator. I thought death was the final severing — the end of a life that had been worn down by betrayal, coercion, and institutional force. But I survived. I experienced that survival as resurrection. I believe God restored me to expose what had been done to me. I returned expecting grief, some acknowledgment that my absence had mattered. Instead, there was silence. Not a tear was shed. I came back to exile.

I found myself living in my car as a disabled person, internally displaced within my own country — a whistleblower seeking asylum from the democracy of my citizenship. This followed three decades of advocacy: standing beside queer people, the vulnerable, and those living with mental illness. I worked to reform systems, to shift policy, to protect others from the very machinery that would later turn on me. Yet when I spoke about what I say happened behind closed doors, I was pushed out. My truth became the justification for my erasure.

I believe an attempted assassination was later thwarted — not out of compassion, but because I had published evidence. Had I died after making those disclosures, the consequences would have been politically explosive. I have named former NDIS minister Bill Shorten because I believe actions taken under his authority sought to have me jailed without proper legal representation and secured an AVO that functioned as political exile, backed by police and institutional force. I experienced media silence as purchased or coerced. In my view, what unfolded was the targeted destruction of a gay, disabled, unprotected whistleblower — a scapegoat rendered expendable. If I had been killed, I believe responsibility would not have rested with one individual, but with every professional who stood by while attrition did its work.

My life is currently under the authority of the NSW Trustee & Guardian, an arrangement I associate with my political exile and with events surrounding the alleged attempt on my life. An NDIS provider, I allege, was compelled to sign a non-disclosure agreement by the Australian Security Intelligence Organisation after confirming how close the situation had come. I was subjected to an allegation that federal police later acknowledged concerned consensual sex. It was not a crime. In my view, the hostility directed at me — rooted in hatred toward a perceived gay man — was weaponised and institutionalised.

Every betrayal, to me, is evidence of spiritual significance. My life is the proof. My survival is the sermon. Through The Gospel of Barran Dodger, I claim what I call divine sovereignty — transforming 35 years of persecution into sacred testimony, and testimony into an incorruptible archive.
